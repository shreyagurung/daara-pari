Mul Ghar

Mul Ghar has been home to our family for a very long time.

The house is made up of two parts- the Kitchen Ghar and the Main House.

The Kitchen Ghar was built by our great-grandmother. It is where the kitchen still is, and where we spend most of our day. The firewood stove, wooden floors and the constant coming and going around the kitchen are still very much part of everyday life here.

The Main House is a newer cottage-like building. Our family lives on the upper floors, while the ground floor has three rooms for guests, arranged around a shared living space.

Common Spaces

The spaces in between.

There is a shared living room in the middle of the house, with a sofa, TV, a small library and a collection of books, mostly on photography, art and travel. There are musical instruments too, and enough space to spend an afternoon doing absolutely nothing in particular.

Outside, the deck has a hammock that is probably the best place to be around here. The lawn on the other side is quieter and open for you to use too.

Around the table

Meals are part
of staying here.

We cook in the Kitchen Ghar over firewood, and meals are usually shared around the same table.

Much of what we cook comes from our farm or from nearby. There is usually something simmering, tea on the stove and someone coming in and out of the kitchen.

This is our Home.
Mul Ghar is our family home. Please respect the shared spaces and treat the house as you would your own, rather than as a hotel.

Nature is part of the experience.
You may encounter insects, bugs, frogs, geckos and other little visitors.

Medical facilities.
The nearest hospital and emergency services are some distance away.

Power cuts.
We do have power backup, but occasional outages are part of village life, especially during heavy rain.

Food & nightlife.
There are no cafés, supermarkets or nightlife nearby. If you're coming to party or looking for a lively night out, this probably isn't the place for you.

Respect the place.
Please be respectful of the local culture, sacred spaces and people in the village. Some places around here hold deep significance for the community, so we ask that you visit them with care and respect.
